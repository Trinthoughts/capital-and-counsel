# Capital & Counsel article template

Use this template when writing a new article. The goal is to keep the publication consistent while leaving the research, argument and writing entirely yours.

## Before writing
- Choose one section: Deals, Private Equity, Financing, or Law & Regulation.
- Pick a specific transaction, legal development, financing issue, or commercial question.
- Research from primary documents where possible, supplemented by reputable reporting.
- Keep a clear distinction between reported facts and your analysis.
- Record the source URLs as you research.

## Article structure
1. Kicker — category + publication date.
2. Headline — specific, editorial and concise.
3. Dek — one sentence explaining the article's central question.
4. Byline — Isaac Lamy · Capital & Counsel.
5. Opening — establish the transaction/question and why it matters.
6. Analysis sections — normally 2–4 sections with descriptive headings.
7. Deal Term — optional plain-English explanation of one important legal/financial term.
8. Conclusion — the main takeaway.
9. Sources — primary documents and reputable reporting.

## Publishing a new article
1. Copy article-template.html.
2. Rename the copy using a short URL-friendly filename, for example: mccormick-unilever-cma.html, shaw-gibbs-financing.html, or sky-itv-regulation.html.
3. Replace every placeholder in the copied file.
4. Save/commit the new HTML file to the repository root.
5. Add the article to the relevant category page.
6. Add it to the Latest analysis section of index.html if it is one of the three newest pieces.
7. If it concerns a tracked transaction, update deal-monitor.html.
8. Check the live site on desktop and mobile.

## Keep the editorial voice
Capital & Counsel should sound analytical, restrained and commercially literate.

Prefer:
- The transaction raises...
- The structure means...
- The public documents show...
- One issue to watch is...

Avoid:
- unsupported claims about motives
- exaggerated language
- presenting opinion as fact
- copying source language at length
- investment recommendations

## File naming
Use lowercase filenames with hyphens. Keep them short and permanent because the filename becomes part of the article URL.

## Source discipline
For important factual claims, identify where the information came from. Prefer company announcements and filings, regulator decisions/case pages, court or government documents, law firm transaction announcements, and reputable financial and legal reporting.

Do not publish a figure, date, ownership detail or transaction term just because it appears in an unsourced secondary post.

## Important
The template is a starting point, not a restriction. If an article genuinely needs a table, timeline, quote, chart or different structure, adapt the HTML while keeping the overall Capital & Counsel design consistent.
